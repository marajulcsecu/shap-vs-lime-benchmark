Post-hoc explanation methods are the standard tool for interpreting high-accuracy
tabular classifiers, and SHAP and LIME dominate practice. They rest on different theory
--- cooperative-game attribution versus a locally fitted linear surrogate --- so they
can disagree about the same prediction, yet practitioners have little guidance on which
to trust when they do. Existing comparisons hold too much fixed: typically one model, or
one dataset, with metrics defined differently from paper to paper and no statistical
testing. We therefore run a controlled comparison that varies all three axes at once:
six classifiers across three public clinical datasets, eighteen model--dataset cells,
under a single leakage-safe protocol with eleven pre-registered hypotheses,
non-parametric tests, Holm correction, effect sizes, bootstrap intervals, and a
random-ranking control that gives every fidelity number a scale. We additionally
decompose explanation stability into three separable sources that single-notion studies
conflate: explainer randomness, input perturbation, and model retraining variance.
Inside that design we also propose and test a hybrid consensus method ---
\emph{fidelity-weighted rank fusion}, which combines the two explainers in proportion to
the faithfulness each demonstrates on a disjoint calibration split --- against two
published fusion baselines. Across \NumCells{} cells and \NumExplained{} explained
instances the two explainers agree substantially but are not interchangeable: at the
median they share four of their five top-ranked features and disagree on the fifth ---
and agreement is lowest on the \emph{easiest} task rather than the hardest. SHAP is the
more faithful, markedly more robust to input perturbation, and roughly
\SpeedFactor{}$\times$ cheaper wherever a tree backend exists, while a bit-identical
explainer proves not to be a stable one: its global ranking still shifts when the model
is refitted on a resample of the same data. Rank fusion, including our fidelity-weighted
rule, failed to beat the better of the two explainers in any cell --- a negative result,
pre-registered as valid, that follows from the level of agreement: fusion needs
complementary inputs and these are not complementary. Every number in this paper is
generated by code from a protocol frozen in version control before any result existed;
the complete pipeline reproduces with one command.
