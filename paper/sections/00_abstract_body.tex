SHAP and LIME dominate post-hoc explanation of tabular classifiers, rest on different
theory, and routinely disagree about the same prediction, yet practitioners have little
controlled evidence on which to trust. Existing comparisons vary one axis at a time,
define fidelity differently from paper to paper, omit a random-ranking control, and do
not state which output space their attributions occupy. We contribute a reproducible
benchmark that varies model family, dataset, and explainer together: two explainers,
three rank-fusion rules, and a random control across six classifiers and three public
clinical datasets --- \NumCells{} cells, \NumExplained{} explained instances --- under
one leakage-safe protocol with eleven hypotheses pre-registered in version control
before any result existed. We also decompose explanation stability into three sources
that single-notion studies conflate. The two methods agree substantially but are not
interchangeable (median Spearman $\rho = \MedianRho{}$, top-5 overlap
\MedianJaccardFive{}); SHAP is more faithful (\aopc{} \AopcShap{} versus \AopcLime{},
against \AopcRandom{} for the control), roughly \SpeedFactor{}$\times$ cheaper, and far
more robust to input perturbation (\LipschitzShap{} versus \LipschitzLime{}); and
cross-refit agreement of \RetrainShap{} shows that a bit-identical explainer is still
not a stable explanation. Rank fusion beat the better of the two in \HSixRejected{} of
\HSixTotal{} cells --- a pre-registered null result. Every number here is generated by
code; the study reproduces with one command.
